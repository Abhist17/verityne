  The Render deployment ships without the detector models (that is what lets

Check these three things first. All three were green when this was written:

      -> must say true

  curl -s -o /dev/null -w "%{http_code}\n" localhost:3000
      -> must say 200

  If either is down:
      Terminal 1:  make backend
      Terminal 2:  make frontend

#########################################################################
PART 1 - THE PROBLEM AND THE SOLUTION          (~1:00, ~150 words)
#########################################################################

[On camera, or on the landing page at localhost:3000.]

  Every KYC vendor will show you a ROC curve and a number that sounds good.

  Here is the problem with that. Anyone onboarding a fake merchant today has
  Stable Diffusion and DeepFaceLab on a consumer laptop. They can generate a
  face that has never existed, put it on a document that scans clean, and
  record a liveness video of a person who does not exist. The single-model
  detectors most systems ship were trained before those tools were free.

  So the question is not "what is your AUC". It is "where does your system
  fail, and do you know?"

  This is Verityne. Six independent detectors, a calibrated fusion layer, and
  a human-readable explanation behind every verdict.

  And one thing you will not see from a vendor: a page listing every belief
  this project held, measured, and turned out to be wrong about. Nine of them.
  I will show you that at the end, because it is the part I would judge us on.

[Beat. Switch to the architecture diagram.]


#########################################################################
PART 2 - ARCHITECTURE                          (~1:00, ~155 words)
#########################################################################

[The architecture diagram, full screen.]

  A packet is four things: a selfie, an ID document, a liveness clip, and the
  timing of how the form was filled.

  Five detectors run concurrently. Selfie deepfake - a pretrained transformer
  plus a frequency head we fitted ourselves. ID forensics - OCR, check digits,
  compression analysis. Liveness - identity drift across video frames.
  Metadata - EXIF and generator tags. And behavioral - keystroke rhythm.

  Face match runs second. Not because it matters less, but because it reuses
  the face crops the first stage already decoded, instead of decoding the same
  images twice.

  Then the path splits.

  Fusion is a calibrated logistic regression: six scores become one.

  Linkage is the other half. We hash every asset and embed every face, and
  check both against every submission we have ever seen. That is how you catch
  a ring instead of an incident.

  And here is the design decision I would point at. Linkage can only ever
  RAISE risk, never lower it. And a face match cannot reject anybody on its
  own - it is capped below the reject line and sent to a human instead,
  because a similarity search over a growing database will eventually
  false-link a real person. A byte-identical file has no such problem, so that
  one is allowed to reject.

[Switch to localhost:3000/verify.]

PART 3 - LIVE DEMO                             (~3:00)
#########################################################################

--- 3A. A genuine applicant passes ------------------------------ (~35s)

[/verify. Click fixture REAL-01. It scores live - 3 to 5 seconds.]

  Let me score a real one first, because a fraud detector that flags everybody
  is not a detector.

  [While it runs:] This is going through the actual API - all six detectors,
  on the GPU, right now.

  [Verdict lands: PASS, low score.]

  Passed. And look at the reasoning - it is not just a number. Natural sensor
  noise, no generative artefacts, the ID's check digit validates, the selfie
  matches the portrait at a similarity that is plausible for a live capture.

  That last one matters. A PERFECT match would be suspicious - it would mean
  the selfie is a copy of the ID photo rather than a fresh capture.


--- 3B. A fraudulent packet, and why ---------------------------- (~55s)

[Click fixture FAKE-02. Let it score live.]

  Now a fraudulent one.

  [Verdict lands: 0.46, REVIEW.]

  Risk 0.46 - REVIEW. Not rejected, not passed. The system declined to decide
  and pushed it to a human, and it says exactly why.

  Three reasons. One: a near-identical image appeared in eleven earlier
  submissions - so this is either a reused asset or a shared template. Two:
  the selfie is softer than its stated resolution implies. It claims 960
  pixels but only carries real detail to about 570, which means it was
  upscaled. Three: natural sensor noise, no generative artefacts.

  Notice the third reason argues AGAINST fraud. We surface the evidence that
  points both ways, because a reviewer who only sees the prosecution case is
  not reviewing anything - they are rubber-stamping.

[Scroll down to the heatmaps and detector list.]

  Below that: where each model actually looked, and all six detector scores
  individually. If you disagree with the verdict, you can see which detector
  drove it.


--- 3C. The same score, a different answer ---------------------- (~30s)

[Hover "Strict" in the MERCHANT POLICY control, top right. A tooltip appears.]

  One more thing on this page. The same evidence does not mean the same
  decision for every business.

  [Tooltip reads: reject 0.60, review 0.25, fraud loss Rs 2.5L]

  This is a crypto exchange - high value, high risk. It rejects at 0.60 where
  the default rejects at 0.80.

  [Hover "Lenient".]

  And this is a gig marketplace - low value, high volume, where friction costs
  more than the fraud does. It rejects at 0.88, and does not even require a
  liveness video.

  Same detectors, same score, different thresholds. That is a config file, not
  a retrain.


--- 3D. Rings, not incidents ------------------------------------ (~35s)

[/threat]

  One fake merchant is an incident. Forty sharing a face is organised, and you
  only see it if you link them.

  These are fraud rings - submissions connected by a shared face or a shared
  file. Five clusters, four of them with byte-identical file reuse.

[/attacks - scroll to a group with REVIEW cards.]

  Same data grouped by attack pattern. And these cards are live - click one
  that is in review...

[Click a REVIEW card. It jumps to /review and scrolls to that exact case.]

  ...and it takes you straight into that case in the reviewer's queue, with
  the evidence already loaded.


--- 3E. The reviewer's queue ------------------------------------ (~25s)

[/review - already here from the click.]

  Twenty-seven cases waiting on a human.

  Look at these three - Farah Sheikh, Nazia Rahman, Sadia Iqbal. Different
  names. Same photograph. Eleven of the twenty-seven carry that badge.

  This is why a face match cannot auto-reject. It is a similarity search, and
  we measured what that costs: at the threshold we originally shipped, a
  genuine applicant false-linked to a stranger ninety-seven percent of the
  time against a five thousand record scan. So it goes to a person.


--- 3F. The numbers ---------------------------------------------- (~35s)

[/metrics]

  Held-out ROC-AUC: 0.753, on an identity-disjoint split - no face that
  trained the fusion layer appears in these numbers.

  That is not a great score, and I want to be precise about why it is the
  honest one. It used to be 0.913. Then an ablation showed our own corpus
  generator had written the label into the EXIF metadata. Seventeen percent of
  the corpus was carrying a fraud label in disguise. We fixed the corpus and
  the number dropped sixteen points.

[Point at the ROC chart.]

  This is the fused curve against each detector alone. The fusion should
  dominate. Where a single detector beats it, our weights are wrong - so we
  draw it rather than crop it.

[Scroll to the cost curve and drag a slider.]

  And this is live: change the assumed fraud loss, and the optimal threshold
  moves. We picked 0.80 from this curve, not by taste.


--- 3G. Close on what we got wrong ------------------------------- (~25s)

[/corrections]

  Last page, and the one I would judge us on.

  Nine things this project believed, measured, and turned out to be wrong
  about. Four fixed. Three still open. Every one linked to the evidence file
  that disproved it.

  Our selfie detector scored 0.997 on diffusion fakes - the one attack class
  we thought we had solved. Tested on four generator families we did not
  create, it is at or below chance on all four. The worst is 0.123, which is
  below fifty percent, which means inverted.

  And our keystroke detector is a perfect 1.000 against every automation
  strategy we had. So we built the attack that beats it - replay a real
  person's recorded typing - and it drops to chance.

  We published both, with the evidence.

  Every vendor shows you a ROC curve. We show you where it fails.


#########################################################################
IF SOMETHING GOES WRONG ON CAMERA
#########################################################################

Verdict takes longer than ~8s
    First score after the backend restarts pays model warm-up. Score one
    fixture BEFORE you start recording to warm it.

A page shows an error box
    Backend died. Check terminal 1. Everything except the landing page reads
    from the API.

Heatmaps do not appear
    They are generated during scoring. If you are on the Render link they do
    not exist - switch to localhost.

You clicked a REJECT card on /attacks and nothing happened
    Correct behaviour. Only REVIEW cases are in the queue, so only those link.
